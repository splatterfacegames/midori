# Flora of south-west Western Australia for Midori

This is desk research to ground Midori's procedural presets (Weber-Penn trees, leaf cards, bark, ground patches) for the native flora of the Perth region and the forested South West, plus the planted exotics of the 1990s Perth streetscape. All dimensions are metres unless a unit is stated (leaf and flower sizes are given in cm or mm as the sources give them); months are southern-hemisphere calendar months. Figures are quoted as ranges where the source gives ranges, and where two sources disagree both are given. "Not found" means no figure was found in the sources consulted; nothing here is estimated. Sources are listed under each species and collected in section 5. Abbreviations: EUCLID = Eucalypts of Australia (Lucid key); Florabase = Western Australian Herbarium; WP = Wikipedia.

## 2. Native species

### 2.1 Forest and woodland trees

### Jarrah — *Eucalyptus marginata*

- Habitat: endemic to the South West, generally where rainfall exceeds 600 mm; typically on soils derived from ironstone (laterite); Florabase gives "grey sand, clay or sandy loam, laterite; hills, rises". Dominant tree of the Darling Range and also the main tree of the coastal sand plain south of Perth, replaced by tuart on coastal limestone.
- Height: Florabase "to 40 m"; EUCLID "to 35 m tall or rarely mallees to 4 m"; WP "sometimes up to 50 m with DBH 3.5 m, more often 40 m with DBH up to 2 m; less commonly a mallee to 3 m". A 1957 Forests Department bulletin (as reported in search results) gives 100-130 ft (30-40 m) with a straight bole of up to 50-60 ft (15-18 m). Crown spread: not found.
- Trunk: on forest sites a straight cylindrical bole about half the total height, with a thin, fairly open crown (robertpowelltrees.org). Lignotuber present.
- Bark: a stringybark; rough, greyish-brown (EUCLID "grey to brown"), vertically grooved, fibrous, "stringy in broad flattish strips", shed in long flat strips; rough down to branches 5-10 cm in diameter.
- Leaves: alternate, lanceolate to falcate, often curved; EUCLID 6-12.5 cm x 1.5-3 cm, petiole 1-2.3 cm (WP 8-13 x 1.5-3 cm); discolorous, shiny dark green above and paler below; often drip-tipped.
- Flowers and fruit: creamy white (Florabase white-cream/pink), 1-2 cm across, in axillary umbels of 7-13 buds (WP 4-8); Florabase Jun-Dec or Jan, EUCLID Aug-Dec. Fruit barrel-shaped to truncate-globose, 0.8-1.6 cm long x 1-1.6 cm wide.
- Fire: lignotuber present in older trees; further fire detail (epicormic resprouting, blackening) not found in the sources read.
- Site variation (second preset recommended): greatest stature on red loams in the wetter south-west (Warren and Murray valleys); lower forests on poorer laterite; "much less tall but stout and spreading" on the Swan Coastal Plain, described in an older survey as "a tree of low height and poor form" in fairly open formation; mallee form near Mt Lesueur and in the Stirling Range. No height figure for the coastal-plain form was found.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/eucalyptus_marginata_subsp._marginata.htm ; https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/5708 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus_marginata ; https://robertpowelltrees.org/?p=830

### Marri — *Corymbia calophylla*

- Habitat: from north of Geraldton to Cape Riche and inland beyond Narrogin; on the Swan Coastal Plain and the Darling Scarp, on flats, hills, wetlands margins and drainage lines, in red-brown clay loams, sandy clays, gravel and grey sandy soils over limestone, granite or laterite. Good specimens indicate the better soils. On the drier coastal plain of its northern range only tuart exceeds it in size.
- Height: EUCLID "usually a tree to 40 m, or mallee-like to about 5 m on poor sites"; WP typically 40 m, can exceed 60 m (largest known 71 m tall, 10.8 m girth). Trunk up to 2 m wide. Crown spread: not found.
- Trunk and branches: "the branches becoming large, thick and rambling" (WP). Lignotuber present.
- Bark: rough to the small branches, tessellated, brown to grey-brown (WP grey-brown to red-brown). A bloodwood: kino ("mayat") oozes from the tree.
- Leaves: ovate to broadly lanceolate; EUCLID 7-19 cm x 2-5.5 cm (WP 9-14 cm x 2.5-4 cm, petiole 1.5-2 cm); flat to slightly undulate, glossy, darker green above and paler below (WP "thick and glossy green on both sides").
- Flowers: terminal branched clusters at the ends of branchlets, held on the outside of the crown; white, rarely pink; WP Dec-May (EUCLID records Jan, Feb, Mar, Sep).
- Fruit ("honky nuts"): urn-shaped (urceolate), EUCLID 1.9-4.4(5) cm long x 1.8-4 cm wide (WP 3-5 cm x 2.5-4 cm) on pedicels 0.7-4 cm; persist on the tree for a year or more.
- Fire: lignotuber. Further fire detail: not found in the sources read.
- Site variation: medium to tall tree over most of the range; mallee on very infertile sites.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/corymbia_calophylla.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corymbia_calophylla

### Karri — *Eucalyptus diversicolor*

- Habitat: only in the high-rainfall zone of the South West (900-1,300 mm a year), where it can form pure stands on acidic sandy/loam soils. Not a Perth tree; stretch content for the Warren region.
- Height: EUCLID "to 80 m"; WP typically 10-60 m, up to 90 m; tallest known living just over 80 m. A large tree ("The Tyrant") is 69 m tall with 11.5 m girth. Crown spread and clear-bole fraction: not found.
- Trunk: tall single straight bole. No lignotuber; epicormic buds under the bark along the stem.
- Bark: smooth throughout, light grey, dark grey and cream to yellow or pale orange, often mottled; shed annually in large plates, short ribbons or small polygonal flakes, so freshly exposed patches contrast with older bark; becomes granular with age; "spectacularly orange in season".
- Leaves: lanceolate, 7-13.5 cm x 1.5-3.7 cm, petiole 1-2 cm; dark green and glossy above, distinctly paler below; "more spreading than pendulous".
- Flowers and fruit: white, axillary; EUCLID records Jan, Apr, May, Aug, Dec. Fruit barrel-shaped, 1-1.2 cm x 0.8-1 cm.
- Fire: no lignotuber; resprouts from epicormic buds.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/eucalyptus_diversicolor.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus_diversicolor

### Tuart — *Eucalyptus gomphocephala*

- Habitat: a narrow coastal corridor of the Swan Coastal Plain, 5-10 km wide, continuous from Yanchep to Busselton (outliers north to Jurien Bay); on coastal sands with limestone at depth.
- Height: EUCLID "to 40 m"; WP usually 10-40 m, tallest known 47 m (Ludlow). Crown spread up to 25 m. Taller at the southern end of the range, smaller at the northern end. DBH: not found.
- Trunk: usually a tall single stem; low and multi-stemmed at the edge of stands under salt wind. No lignotuber.
- Bark: rough throughout (trunk and main branches), finely fibrous, box-like, grey, breaking into small flaky pieces; tessellated on large old stems.
- Leaves: lanceolate to falcate, often curved, 9-18 cm (EUCLID 10-18) x 1.5-3 cm; thin, glossy, light green, concolorous to slightly discolorous.
- Flowers and fruit: white to cream, axillary, Jan-Apr. Fruit bell-shaped to obconical, 1-1.9 cm long x 1.2-1.8 cm wide.
- Fire: no lignotuber; epicormic buds along the stem.
- Site variation (second preset recommended): tall forest tree at Ludlow versus broad spreading open-grown tree (crown to 25 m) and low multi-stemmed coastal form.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/eucalyptus_gomphocephala.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus_gomphocephala

### Wandoo — *Eucalyptus wandoo*

- Habitat: from Morawa south through the Darling Range to the Pallinup River, on sandy loams, clay loams and stony soils; forms open woodland of widely spaced trees over a sparse understorey of shrubs, grasses and herbs.
- Height: EUCLID "to 18 m"; WP typically 3-25 m, sometimes 30-31 m. DBH 0.8-1.0 m. Crown spread: not found.
- Trunk: usually straight; bole is 50-65% of total height. Lignotuber inconspicuous, can become huge in old trees.
- Bark: smooth throughout on mature trees, white (powdery or not), often mottled with patches of white, grey or light brown; old darker bark persists as scattered loosely held flakes and is shed in irregular slabs. Saplings and coppice have rough fibrous yellow-brown bark.
- Leaves: lanceolate or slightly falcate, 7.5-12.5 cm x 1-2.8 cm; dull, grey-green to blue-grey (rarely glossy green), concolorous.
- Flowers and fruit: white, axillary, buds in groups of 9-17; EUCLID records Jan-Apr, Jun, Aug, Oct. Fruit long-obconical to cylindrical, 0.6-1 cm x 0.5-0.6 cm.
- Fire: regenerates from the lignotuber's epicormic buds after loss of the crown.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/eucalyptus_wandoo_subsp._wandoo.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus_wandoo

### Flooded gum — *Eucalyptus rudis*

- Habitat: stream banks, floodplains and swampy ground on silty soils with clay subsoil; the eucalypt of the Swan and Canning river flats and lake margins.
- Height: EUCLID "to 20 m"; WP 5-20 m. Crown spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk: "usually short and has a poor form with a wide-spreading crown". Lignotuber present.
- Bark: rough, fibrous, dark and light grey, box-type over part or all of the trunk and larger branches, tessellated on older trunks; smooth greyish above (occasionally smooth throughout, especially north of Perth where it intergrades with river red gum).
- Leaves: lanceolate to curved, 7-18 cm x 1-3.5 cm, petiole 1.3-3 cm; dull to slightly glossy, green.
- Flowers and fruit: white, axillary, buds in 7s-11s, mainly Jul-Nov. Fruit bell- to cup-shaped or hemispherical, 4-7 mm long x 7-10 mm wide with strongly protruding valves.
- Seasonal look: often heavily attacked by leaf miners, leaf-blister sawflies and lerps in spring; the crown regenerates in late spring and summer.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/eucalyptus_rudis_subsp._rudis.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus_rudis

### Red tingle — *Eucalyptus jacksonii*

- Habitat: restricted to well-drained loams in subcoastal river valleys and hillsides near Walpole (Walpole-Nornalup National Park). Stretch content only.
- Height: EUCLID "to 65 m"; WP typically 8-45 m, tallest known living 52 m (some references estimate to 75 m). Base circumference of very old buttressed trees up to 24 m. Crown spread: not found.
- Trunk: massive, shallow-rooted, prominently buttressed base; old bases commonly hollowed by fire (the "Giant Tingle Tree").
- Bark: rough to the small branches, thick, stringy and furrowed, somewhat brittle, grey over red-brown.
- Crown: "dense and compact, forming a heavy canopy".
- Leaves: ovate to lanceolate, 6-9.5(11.5) cm x 1.2-2.5(3) cm, petiole 1-2 cm; only slightly glossy, dark green above, paler below.
- Flowers and fruit: white, axillary, buds in 7s, Jan-Mar. Fruit truncate-globose to short barrel-shaped, 0.8-1.1 cm x 0.7-1 cm.
- Fire: fire-hollowed, blackened buttresses are the signature; resprouting mode not found in the sources read.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/eucalyptus_jacksonii.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus_jacksonii

### WA peppermint — *Agonis flexuosa*

- Habitat: coastal heath to woodland and forest on white or grey sand, laterite and limestone; coastal dunes, granite outcrops, limestone. Widely planted in Perth as a street and park tree (see section 3).
- Height and spread: Florabase "tree or shrub to 10 m"; WP typically 10 m, sometimes a wind-swept mallee or almost prostrate shrub; the Victorian horticultural flora gives a maximum of about 15 m (as reported in search results). Perth council street-tree guides give 10 x 10 m (Gosnells), 8.2 m high x 9.4 m wide (Victoria Park) and 8 m high x 6 m spread (Wanneroo).
- Trunk and bark: bark fibrous, brown and furrowed (some sources say dark brown or red-brown); old trunks often show a spiral twist in the bark.
- Crown: dense, domed, weeping; "looks remarkably like the weeping willow from a distance"; branchlets slender, often twisted or zig-zagged, pendulous.
- Leaves: very narrowly elliptic, 8-13.5 cm x 3.5-12 mm, sessile or petiole to 5 mm; pendulous. Colour: "soft green" (Kwinana guide); gloss not found.
- Flowers and fruit: small white flowers (petals 3-6 mm) in clusters 10-15 mm wide strung along the branchlets, Jul-Dec; capsules 2.5-4 mm in clusters 6-10 mm wide.
- Fire: not found in the sources read.
- Site variation: tall weeping tree inland and in cultivation versus wind-pruned mallee or near-prostrate shrub on exposed coast.
- Sources: https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/5316 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agonis_flexuosa ; https://www.gosnells.wa.gov.au/City-Services/Community-Safety/In-your-street/Street-trees/Approved-Street-Trees/WA-Weeping-Peppermint ; https://www.victoriapark.wa.gov.au/documents/623/leafy-streets-tree-selection-guide-2025 ; https://www.joondalup.wa.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Tree-Information-Sheets.pdf ; https://www.northernbeaches.nsw.gov.au/environment/trees/willow-myrtle

### Common sheoak — *Allocasuarina fraseriana*

- Habitat: jarrah woodland and open forest and sand dunes in near-coastal regions between Perth and Albany, on lateritic soils and white, grey or yellow sand; a regular companion of banksia and jarrah on the coastal plain and scarp.
- Height: 5-15 m (Florabase and WP). DBH 0.5-1 m. Crown spread: not found.
- Trunk and bark: erect tree; bark fibrous, reddish-brown (Florabase).
- Foliage: no true leaves; green needle-like branchlets up to 30 cm long, "more or less erect", jointed every 7-15 mm, 0.8-1.3 mm thick, with whorls of 6-8 scale teeth. Fallen branchlets make a soft needle litter under the tree.
- Flowers and cones: male spikes 3-8 cm long (Florabase "fl. brown"), May-Oct; female cones are woody cones 1.5-4 cm long x 1.5-2.2 cm in diameter.
- Fire: not found in the sources read.
- Sources: https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/1728 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allocasuarina_fraseriana

### Swamp sheoak — *Casuarina obesa*

- Habitat: moist, often brackish or saline places along rivers, creeks and salt lakes, on sand or clay; lines the Swan and Canning estuary shores.
- Height: Florabase 1.5-10 m; WP 3-15 m. Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: small tree or shrub that forms root suckers (so it stands in thickets); bark corky and deeply fissured.
- Foliage: branchlets drooping or spreading to erect, up to 30 cm long, articles 8-14 mm long x 0.9-1.4 mm wide, with whorls of 12-16 teeth. Colour: not found (the common name of its eastern relative, C. glauca, refers to grey-green).
- Flowers and cones: dioecious; male spikes 1.5-6 cm; cones cylindrical, 1-2 cm long x 1-1.5 cm in diameter. Florabase: flowers Jan-Dec.
- Sources: https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/1742 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casuarina_obesa

### WA Christmas tree, moodjar — *Nuytsia floribunda*

- Habitat: sandplains, slopes and the base of rock outcrops on white, grey or yellow sand, sandy loam, gravel, laterite and limestone, throughout the South West; once common across the Swan Coastal Plain. A root hemiparasite, so it stands among other plants (banksia woodland, paddock edges) and often survives clearing as isolated trees.
- Height: tree or shrub to 10 m (Florabase, WP); the Victorian horticultural flora says "to 7 m" (as reported in search results). Trunk up to 1.2 m in diameter (WP). Spread: not found.
- Trunk and bark: stout trunk of layered wood and bark; bark rough, grey-brown. Suckers from long roots, so it can appear as a grove.
- Leaves: opposite or nearly so, lanceolate, 2.5-10 cm long, stalkless (Victorian flora, as reported in search results). Width and colour: not found.
- Flowers (the signature): vivid yellow-orange, Oct-Jan (Florabase Oct-Dec or Jan), in terminal racemes of up to 50 three-flowered clusters; the inflorescence on each flowering stem may be up to 1 m long, so the whole crown turns orange.
- Fire: the layered trunk "allows the tree to withstand fire" (WP).
- Sources: https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/2401 ; https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/21336 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuytsia_floribunda ; https://hortflora.rbg.vic.gov.au/taxon/ad9b1570-5340-11e7-b82b-005056b0018f (not fetched directly; figures as reported in search results)

### 2.2 Banksias

### Firewood banksia — *Banksia menziesii*

- Habitat: deep sands of the Swan Coastal Plain and Geraldton Sandplains, Waroona to Kalbarri; a dominant of banksia low woodland and jarrah-banksia woodland around Perth; stopped to the east by the heavy soils of the Darling Scarp.
- Height: WP gnarled tree to 10 m, or a spreading 1-3 m shrub at its northern limits; Florabase "tree or shrub, 1.3-7 m". Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: gnarled; trunk greyish, sometimes with shades of brown or pink; bark rough, 2-3 cm thick, breaks away easily.
- Leaves: oblong, truncate at the tip, 8-25 cm long x up to 4 cm wide, margins finely serrate with 1-2 mm triangular teeth; dull grey-green, new growth paler grey-green and finely downy (rusty-brown hairs on new stems).
- Flower spikes: terminal, ovoid to cylindrical, 4-12 cm high x 7-8 cm wide; typically two-coloured red or pink and yellow (also chocolate, bronze, yellow, white and greenish variants); WP autumn and winter, peaking May-Jul (Florabase Feb-Oct). Old flowers fall quickly, leaving a bare cone with up to 25 large beaked follicles.
- Fire: resprouts from a lignotuber or epicormic buds; follicles open only after being burnt.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banksia_menziesii ; https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/1834

### Slender (candlestick) banksia — *Banksia attenuata*

- Habitat: the most widely distributed western banksia, Kalbarri to Cape Leeuwin and east to Fitzgerald River; co-dominant with B. menziesii on Perth's sands.
- Height: tree to 10 m; stunted multi-stemmed shrub 0.4-2 m in the drier north. Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: solid trunk, "generally wavy or bent"; bark 1-2 cm thick, crumbly, orange-grey, red-brown underneath.
- Leaves: long, narrow, linear, 4-27 cm x 0.5-1.6 cm, with V- or U-shaped serrations along the whole margin; shiny green; new growth pale grey-green in late spring and summer.
- Flower spikes: terminal on 1-3 year old branches, held upright above the foliage like candles; up to 25-30 cm tall x 5 cm wide; bright green in bud, brilliant yellow in flower (spring into summer), then fading to brown and finally grey, with the dead flowers persisting against the spike; a few dark furry follicles 2-3.5 cm long develop.
- Fire: resprouter, from the lignotuber or from epicormic buds in the fire-tolerant trunk.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banksia_attenuata

### Bull banksia — *Banksia grandis*

- Habitat: woodland and heath on the coastal plain from Mount Lesueur to Cape Leeuwin and east to Cape Riche; common as the small-tree layer of the jarrah forest on the Darling Range.
- Height: usually 5-10 m, sometimes 15 m; a stunted spreading shrub near the south coast and among granite. Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: trunks "short, stout and often crooked", with rough grey bark; fire-resistant main stem with thick bark.
- Leaves (the signature): very large, 10-45 cm long x 3-11 cm wide on a 1-3.5 cm petiole, cut to the midrib into 8-12 large triangular lobes on each side; shiny dark green above, softly hairy beneath.
- Flower spikes: 10-40 cm long x 7-9 cm wide, pale yellow with cream styles, Oct-Jan; old flowers fall early, leaving a massive bare cone.
- Fire: thick-barked, fire-resistant stem; resprouting detail not found in the sources read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banksia_grandis

### Swamp banksia — *Banksia littoralis*

- Habitat: along creeks and rivers and in low-lying, seasonally damp swamps and depressions of the coastal South West; with paperbarks and flooded gum around Perth's wetlands.
- Height: typically 1.5-12 m, sometimes to 25 m. Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: rough, crumbly bark; woolly-hairy young stems.
- Leaves: in whorls, linear, 7-23 cm x 4-18 mm, serrated mostly in the upper half; petiole 5-10 mm. Colour: not found in the source read.
- Flower spikes: cylindrical, 7-20 cm long x 6-7 cm wide, yellow, Mar-Jul; up to 200 small follicles per cone, the old flowers falling.
- Fire: resprouts from epicormic buds.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banksia_littoralis

### 2.3 Wetland and river-edge paperbarks

### Swamp paperbark — *Melaleuca rhaphiophylla*

- Habitat: sandy or clay soils near watercourses, in swamps and saltmarshes, Kalbarri to Albany; the common paperbark of Perth's freshwater lakes and river edges, often standing in water in winter.
- Height: large shrub or small tree to 10 m. Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: often multi-stemmed, with a bushy, dense crown; bark greyish, papery, in layers.
- Leaves: soft, needle-like, alternate, 8-40 mm long x 0.5-1.5 mm wide, round or oval in section, with a hooked point. Colour: not found.
- Flowers and fruit: white to cream bottlebrush-like spikes up to 40 mm long x 27 mm across at the branch ends, sporadically, mainly May-Jan; woody cup-shaped capsules 3-6 mm long x 5-6 mm across clustered along the stems.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melaleuca_rhaphiophylla

### Saltwater paperbark — *Melaleuca cuticularis*

- Habitat: saline wetlands, swamps and estuaries; common in coastal regions south of Perth to Israelite Bay; fringes the Swan estuary and salt lakes.
- Height: usually a shrub 1-7 m, sometimes a tree to 12 m. Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: very white papery bark; "rigid and tortuous branches", giving a twisted, angular silhouette.
- Leaves: linear to oblong, 4-12 mm long x 1.5-3 mm wide, grey-green to dark green.
- Flowers and fruit: white or cream, in threes at the branch ends, Sep-Jan; solitary woody capsules 6-11 mm long, star-shaped end-on.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melaleuca_cuticularis

### Modong, stout paperbark — *Melaleuca preissiana*

- Habitat: seasonally wet sands and swamps of the coastal South West, Jurien Bay to Albany; typical of damplands within banksia woodland and of peaty mound springs on the Swan Coastal Plain.
- Height: shrub or small tree, sometimes to 10-15 m. Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: papery bark, or sometimes pale hard bark.
- Leaves: alternate, flat, narrowly elliptic, 6-14 mm long x 0.7-2.1 mm wide, tapering to a point. Colour: not found.
- Flowers and fruit: usually white (sometimes cream or yellow) spikes up to 20 mm in diameter at the branch ends, Aug-Mar; capsules 2.5-3 mm.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melaleuca_preissiana

### 2.4 Understorey and character plants

### Grass tree, balga — *Xanthorrhoea preissii*

- Habitat: coastal plain, ranges and near watercourses from Geraldton to Albany, on grey to black sands, loam, gravelly clay, laterite and granite; a dominant of marri-balga woodlands on the eastern Swan Coastal Plain and throughout the jarrah forest.
- Size: tree-like monocot to 5 m high overall, trunk "to over 3 m" (Florabase); mature trunk diameter typically 25–45 cm (Flora of Australia / Bedford 1986).
- Trunk: a caudex built of packed old leaf bases, usually charred black by fire; sometimes unbranched, branching after damage to the growing point or naturally (genus-level statement), so old plants carry two or more heads. The leaf bases show annual colour banding.
- Leaves: rigid, linear, diamond-quadrangular in cross-section, 0.8–1.5 m long × 2–4 mm wide (Flora of Australia / Florabase profile 1256), emerging in a dense radial sphere. Leaf production is continuous, 2.5–3.2 leaves a day from late spring to autumn and 0.5 a day in winter; leaves live more than 2 years (Lamont et al.).
- Skirt: the plant "holds its thick, dead leaves around its stem", which insulates it against fire; fire burns the leaves and blackens the trunk (genus page).
- Flower spike: scape (bare stalk) 0.6–1.0 m (15–30 mm diameter) plus flowering spike 1.5–2.5 m × 25–45 mm diameter (Florabase / Flora of Australia), carrying sessile creamy-white flowers, Jun or Aug to Dec; the dark woody spike persists after fruiting.
- Growth rate: trunk elongation typically 10–20 mm a year (range 5–50 mm) from leaf-base band analysis; a WA agriculture department page gives 1–2 cm a year.
- Fire: survives fire (leaves burn, trunk blackens); flowering is more profuse when stimulated by fire.
- Sources: https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/1256 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthorrhoea_preissii ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthorrhoea ; https://publish.csiro.au/BT/BT03127 (abstract, via search results) ; https://profiles.ala.org.au/opus/foa/profile/Xanthorrhoea%20preissii

### Zamia — *Macrozamia riedlei*

- Habitat: lateritic soils, typically in jarrah forest; also on the Swan Coastal Plain.
- Size: 0.5-3 m high overall, "small, usually trunkless" (Florabase). Stem mostly below ground; where emergent, barrel-shaped, up to 0.5 m high and 25-40 cm in diameter (llifle); WP says an above-ground trunk up to 2 m can form.
- Fronds: 12-30 in the crown, glossy mid- to dark green, flat, arching, each 1.2-2.2 m long (llifle 1-2.2 m) with a petiole 18-50 cm.
- Leaflets: 92-150 per frond, evenly arranged either side of the rachis; width (5-)7.5-11(-15) mm; length given by llifle as "(23-)35-30(-48) cm", which contains a typo (the range is roughly 23-48 cm). Narrow, with a pale basal gland.
- Cones: in the centre of the crown, Sep-Oct. Male cones 29-41 cm long x 11-15 cm in diameter (another key gives 20-40 cm), staying green; female cones 25-50 cm long x 12-25 cm wide (llifle 25-35 x 14-18 cm), up to 14 kg, breaking up to show large bright red fleshy seeds.
- Fire: not found in the sources read.
- Sources: https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/85 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrozamia_riedlei ; https://www.llifle.com/Encyclopedia/PALMS_AND_CYCADS/Family/Cycadaceae/31964/Cycas_riedlei

### Red-and-green kangaroo paw — *Anigozanthos manglesii*

- Habitat: white, yellow or grey sand and sandy loam; open forest, banksia woodland and heath, including bushland around Perth.
- Size: rhizomatous perennial herb, 0.2–1.1 m high in flower (Florabase).
- Leaves: a fan of grey-green, strap-like (linear) leaves from ground level, 30–60 cm long × 10–25 mm wide (Florabase / Flora of Australia; ANPSA says the leaf clump is about 30 cm high).
- Flower stems: reddish woolly scapes; up to 1.2 m (WP), "up to 80 cm" (ANPSA), about 1 m (ANBG). Spent stalks persist after the season.
- Flowers: velvety, with a bright red base and stalk and bottle-green curved tube 60–100 mm long (Flora of Australia), opening in sequence from the lowest flower upward; Aug–Nov (ANBG Aug–Oct in the wild).
- Fire: regenerates from underground rhizome after fire, often flowering prolifically in the following spring.
- Sources: https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/1411 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anigozanthos_manglesii ; https://anpsa.org.au/plant_profiles/anigozanthos-manglesii/ ; https://profiles.ala.org.au/opus/foa/profile/Anigozanthos%20manglesii

### Harsh hakea — *Hakea prostrata* (and pincushion hakea, *H. laurina*, planted)

- H. prostrata habitat: Geraldton to Esperance on sandy soils often over laterite, loam and gravel; hillslopes, granite outcrops and stabilised coastal dunes (Darling Scarp and coastal limestone around Perth).
- H. prostrata form: erect to spreading or prostrate shrub, 1-3 m high, with spreading branchlets; no lignotuber. Leaves oblong-obovate, stem-clasping, with prickly-toothed margins and a central vein (dimensions not found). Flowers white-cream, sweetly scented, in axillary clusters, Jul-Oct.
- H. laurina (native further south-east, Narrogin to Esperance; widely planted as an ornamental and street shrub): upright shrub or small tree 2.5-6 m high x 3-5 m wide, smooth grey bark, no lignotuber. Leaves flat, entire, lance- to egg-shaped, slightly blue-green, 7-21 cm x 0.6-2.9 cm. Flowers in globular "pincushions" of 120-190 flowers, dark pink to red with cream-white styles 14-19.5 mm long, in the leaf axils, Apr-Jul. Woody fruit 2.2-3.8 cm x 1.6-2.3 cm.
- Sources: https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/2197 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakea_prostrata ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakea_laurina

### Coojong, golden wreath wattle — *Acacia saligna*

- Habitat: throughout the South West, north to the Murchison River; a natural coloniser of disturbed soil, so typical of road verges, railway reserves, dune swales and river banks around Perth.
- Form: small, dense, spreading tree with a short trunk and a weeping habit, up to 8 m tall. Spread: not found. Grows over 1 m a year when young.
- Phyllodes: 8-25 cm long. Width and colour: not found in the source read (one common name is "blue-leafed wattle").
- Flowers: bright yellow spherical heads in groups of up to ten, late winter and early spring. Fruit a legume.

### Stinkwood — *Jacksonia sternbergiana* and grey stinkwood — *J. furcellata*

- J. sternbergiana: erect or weeping shrub or small tree, 1.5-5 m high x 1-3 m wide; leafless, with dull green branches whose end branchlets (4-92 mm long, 1-2 mm wide) are sharply pointed; yellow-orange pea flowers with red markings, 8-10 mm, scattered along the branches all year; hairy pods 10-15.5 mm. Widespread from Northampton south through the Swan Coastal Plain.

### Coastal spinifex — *Spinifex longifolius* (beach spinifex) and *S. hirsutus* (hairy spinifex)

### Thick-leaved fan-flower — *Scaevola crassifolia*

- Leaves egg-shaped to round, thick, mostly toothed, 18-80 mm x 3-43 mm. Colour: not found.

### Coastal daisy-bush — *Olearia axillaris*

## 3. Planted exotics of the 1990s Perth streetscape (not native — listed separately on purpose)

None of the plants in this section is native to the Perth region. They are the planted trees of streets, parks, foreshores and school grounds. Two of them (river red gum, lemon-scented gum) are Australian but not local; WA peppermint is a South West native used as a street tree and is described in section 2. Historical context for a 1990s setting: the City of South Perth states that about 80% of its street trees were planted by returning war veterans, the main species being jacarandas, cape lilacs, Queensland box and WA peppermints, with the bulk of its weeping peppermints, cape lilacs, coral trees and Queensland box planted about six decades before its plan was written; the City of Stirling says brush box was "commonly planted from the 1940s through to the 1980s" and still makes up more than 15% of its street trees. By the 1990s these were therefore mature trees. (Sources: https://southperth.wa.gov.au/docs/default-source/1-residents/services/street-trees/street-tree-management-plan.pdf and https://southperth.wa.gov.au/docs/default-source/5-future/our-environment/urban-forest/urban-forest-strategy.pdf, both as reported in search results; https://www.stirling.wa.gov.au/waste-and-environment/trees/faqs-and-useful-documents)

Imperial figures from North American sources are converted to metric with the original in brackets.

### Jacaranda — *Jacaranda mimosifolia*

- Origin and Perth use: native to south-central South America. In Perth a street and park tree: a grove planted in 1921 stands in the south-east corner of Hyde Park; Applecross (Ardross Street, Nisbet Road, Tain Street) is the best-known jacaranda suburb, and Subiaco has an Old Jacaranda Way; a main species of the post-war South Perth street plantings.
- Height and spread: WP "up to 20 m"; City of Gosnells street-tree list 10-15 m high x 10-15 m wide; City of Melville "up to 15 m"; NCSU 7.6-15 m high x 4.6-7.6 m wide (25-50 ft x 15-25 ft). No source read gave a spread greater than the height, so "wider than tall" is not confirmed; the Perth council figure is height and spread about equal.
- Trunk and bark: trunk about 0.5 m in diameter at maturity (20 in); young trees have a long unbranched trunk. Bark thin, grey-brown and smooth when young, becoming finely scaly (WP); NCSU describes mature bark as dark grey, bumpy, with fine vertical fissures. Twigs slender, slightly zigzag, light reddish-brown.
- Crown: ascending, irregular; a vase-shaped crown of branches (NCSU).
- Leaves: opposite, bipinnate, fern-like, up to 45 cm long (juvenile leaves to about 1 m / 39 in); 13-31 pinnae; leaflets 3-12 mm long x 1-4 mm wide (monograph figure via search results; WP "little more than 1 cm"), small and pointed. Green; fresh spring leaves light green; turn yellow in winter before falling (Melville).
- Flowers: violet / lilac-blue / purple-blue tubular flowers up to 5 cm long in terminal panicles about 30 cm long, lasting up to two months.
- Flowering in Perth: sources differ slightly. Time Out: mid-October to late November; Urban List: from mid-October, peaking mid-November; BHG: starts November, peaks December; RAC: Ardross Street is "a sea of purple each November".
- Seasonal states: semi-deciduous (Gosnells, Melville). Leaves yellow in winter and drop; the tree "blooms while leafless ... before new leaves emerge" (NCSU); "the blooms are sometimes present before the fresh, new, light green leaves appear in spring" (Florida extension, via search results). So: (1) summer to autumn, full fern-like green crown; (2) winter, yellowing and thinning; (3) late winter to spring, bare or nearly bare; (4) mid-October to December, covered in purple, often on bare branches, with new light-green leaves emerging during or after flowering. The exact Perth months of leaf fall were not found.
- Fallen-flower carpet: implied by the "purple haze"/"sea of purple" descriptions of whole streets, but no source read states its extent; treat the purple ground layer under the crown as an art decision.
- Seed pods: flat, round, woody capsules about 5 cm across (WP; 5-8 cm also given; NCSU 2.5-7.5 cm / 1-3 in), brown, splitting to release winged seeds.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacaranda_mimosifolia ; https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/jacaranda-mimosifolia/ ; https://www.gosnells.wa.gov.au/City-Services/Community-Safety/In-your-street/Street-trees/Approved-Street-Trees ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Perth ; via search results only: https://sweetgum.nybg.org/science/vh/monographs-details/?irn=11880 , https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/ST317 , https://www.melvillecity.com.au/CityOfMelville/media/Documents-and-PDF-s/Street-Tree-Species-Guide-Jacaranda-mimosifolia-Jacaranda.pdf , https://www.timeout.com/australia/things-to-do/the-best-place-to-see-jacarandas-in-australia , https://www.theurbanlist.com/perth/a-list/jacaranda-walks-perth , https://www.bhg.com.au/where-to-see-jacaranda-trees-australia , https://rac.com.au/horizons/explore/jacaranda-festival-event-applecross-perth

### Norfolk Island pine — *Araucaria heterophylla*

- Origin and Perth use: endemic to Norfolk Island. The signature tree of Cottesloe: the heritage-listed Norfolk Island Pine Precinct covers Curtin Avenue, Railway, Forrest, Loma and John Streets and parts of Jarrad, Marmion and Broome Streets, Marine Parade and the Cottesloe Beach foreshore; the first were planted in 1903-1904 (John Street and Broome Street), so they were about 90 years old in the 1990s. The first tree planted in Kings Park was also a Norfolk Island pine.
- Height and spread: 50-65 m in habitat (WP). In cultivation 18-30 m high (60-100 ft) with a spread of 3.7-6 m (12-20 ft), up to 18 m (60 ft) in old trees (NCSU). Heights of the Cottesloe trees: not found. DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: one straight vertical trunk, upright "even in the face of incessant onshore winds"; bark grey-brown, falling in fine scales (WP; NCSU "rough and dark brown").
- Branching: regular tiers (whorls) of five almost horizontal or slightly oblique branches, "the plane of each floor a perfect pentagon" (WP; NCSU "each whorl typically has five branches"); branchlets in regular whorls of four to seven along each branch, giving a flat herringbone frond. Outline symmetrical and triangular with widely spaced tiers; may become less symmetrical at maturity. Vertical spacing between whorls and the branch angle in degrees: not found.
- Leaves: juvenile leaves soft, awl-shaped, 10-15 mm long and about 1 mm thick; adult leaves incurved, scale-like, 5-10 mm long x 2-4 mm broad, overlapping along the branchlets; dark green.
- Cones: squat, globose, 10-12 cm long x 12-14 cm in diameter, in the upper crown.
- Pruning: none found; the form is left natural.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araucaria_heterophylla ; https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/araucaria-heterophylla/ ; https://inherit.dplh.wa.gov.au/Public/Inventory/PrintSingleRecord/843d96d4-c097-4a31-921b-05820705c472 ; https://www.cottesloe.wa.gov.au/explore-cottesloe/about-cottesloe/history-and-heritage.aspx (via search results) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_Western_Australia

### London plane — *Platanus x acerifolia* (syn. *P. x hispanica*)

- Origin and Perth use: a hybrid of oriental plane and American sycamore, "used extensively as a street tree" in Sydney, Melbourne, Adelaide and Perth. In Perth: the avenue around the Hyde Park lakes, planted 1897-1899; a heritage-listed group of 27 mature planes lining Victoria Avenue in the city.
- Height and spread: 20-40 m, exceptionally 50 m, trunk to 10 m in circumference (WP); 21-30 m high x 18-23 m wide (70-100 ft x 60-75 ft; NCSU). Measured Perth trees (Victoria Avenue, heritage record): tallest 20-22 m high, trunk circumference 2.3-2.8 m (about 0.73-0.89 m diameter), canopy spread 17-19 m.
- Trunk and bark: single trunk; pyramidal when young, becoming open and spreading with age. Bark light brown to pale grey-green outer layer exfoliating in irregular plates to show creamy-olive inner bark, giving a camouflage mottle of brown, grey-green, olive and cream; some trees buff-brown and not exfoliating.
- Leaves: deciduous; thick, stiff, palmately 3-5 lobed, maple-like; blade 10-20 cm long x 12-25 cm broad, petiole 3-10 cm; medium to dark green, paler beneath; autumn colour yellow-brown.
- Fruit: spherical seed balls 2-3 cm across (NCSU 2.5-3.8 cm), usually two per hanging stalk, brown, persisting on the bare tree through winter.
- Seasonal states: bare in winter with hanging seed balls and conspicuous mottled trunk; new leaves in spring; dense shade in summer; yellow-brown leaf fall in autumn.
- Pruning: often pollarded: "much shorter with stunted, club-like branches", repruned every year. Whether Perth's street planes were pollarded or high-pruned over roads: not found (a 1986 National Trust assessment called the Victoria Avenue trees "poorly managed").
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_plane ; https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/platanus-x-acerifolia/ ; https://inherit.dplh.wa.gov.au/Public/Inventory/PrintSingleRecord/e6858e10-6d8b-4eac-a684-1340f1790629 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Perth

### Moreton Bay fig — *Ficus macrophylla*

- Origin and Perth use: eastern Australian rainforest strangler fig, widely planted in public parks since early settlement. In Perth: historic trees on the lawn area of Hyde Park.
- Height and spread: to 60 m in rainforest (WP); in cultivation 30-40 m high with a spread of 20-30 m (Botanic Gardens of South Australia). A measured park tree (Swan Hill, Victoria, 1987): 28.5 m high, 11 m girth, 44 m spread (via search results). Trunk diameter to 2.4 m.
- Trunk and roots: massive, usually single-stemmed in parks, with thick prominent buttressing: "massive structures of buttress roots spreading out from the trunk"; one Sydney tree has buttress roots "as tall as the average man"; roots tend to run along the surface. Horizontal extent of the buttresses: not found. May drop aerial roots from the crown (mainly the Lord Howe form).
- Bark: thin, smooth, grey when young, darkening with age; grey-brown and blemished on old trunks.
- Crown: wide-spreading, dense, round; heavily branched, "spreading masses of foliage"; low limb height not quantified in the sources read.
- Leaves: evergreen, alternate, large, elliptic to oblong, leathery, 15-30 cm long (width not found); shiny dark green above, paler or rust-coloured below. (WP notes its undersides are greener than those of the Port Jackson fig.)
- Fruit: figs 2-2.5 cm in diameter, green turning purple with lighter spots, all year, most abundant February to May; messy fruit drop under the crown.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus_macrophylla ; https://plantselector.botanicgardens.sa.gov.au/Plants/Details/17878 ; https://www.centennialparklands.com.au/Visit/Environment/Trees-shrubs-plants/Moreton-Bay-Fig ; https://dendro.cnre.vt.edu/dendrology/syllabus/factsheet.cfm?ID=740 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Perth ; https://hortflora.rbg.vic.gov.au/taxon/ad8d6934-5340-11e7-b82b-005056b0018f (via search results)

### Port Jackson fig — *Ficus rubiginosa*

- Origin and Perth use: eastern Australia. In Perth: one on the lawn area of Hyde Park alongside the Moreton Bay figs. Further Perth plantings: not found.
- Height and spread: up to 30 m or more and "nearly as wide", though rarely over 10 m in the Sydney region. Trunk to 1.5 m in diameter.
- Trunk and bark: buttressed trunk; bark yellow-brown.
- Crown: "a spreading, densely-shading tree"; smaller and more compact than the Moreton Bay fig, suited to car parks and suburban streets.
- Leaves: ovate to oval, 4-19.3 cm long x 1.25-13.2 cm wide; glossy green above, undersides rusty (fine rusty hairs), the easiest distinction from Moreton Bay fig together with the smaller leaf.
- Fruit: figs 4-10 mm across (also given as 7-17 mm long), yellow ripening red, in pairs, all year, mostly spring and summer.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ficus_rubiginosa ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Perth

### River red gum — *Eucalyptus camaldulensis*

- Origin and Perth use: the most widespread eucalypt in Australia, along inland watercourses; native to parts of Western Australia but not to Perth, where it is planted and has naturalised. A named specimen, the Queen's Tree, was planted in Kings Park in 1954. North of Perth it intergrades with the local flooded gum.
- Height and spread: typically 20 m, sometimes 45 m (WP); EUCLID "to 40 m". Spread and DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: bark smooth, white, cream and pale grey with patches of yellow, pink or brown; often loose rough slabs or grey flakes at the base. Limbs "often fall without warning".
- Leaves: lanceolate to curved, 5-30 cm long (EUCLID 8-30) x 0.7-3.2 cm wide, petiole 0.8-3.3 cm; dull green or grey-green, the same on both sides.
- Flowers and fruit: white, axillary, buds in 7s-11s, mainly summer; fruit hemispherical, 2-5 mm long x 4-10 mm wide, valves strongly exserted.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/eucalyptus_camaldulensis_subsp._camaldulensis.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucalyptus_camaldulensis

### Lemon-scented gum — *Corymbia citriodora*

- Origin and Perth use: north-eastern Australia. In Perth: the Fraser Avenue entrance to Kings Park, planted in 1938 to replace diseased red-flowering gums; it has since become an environmental weed in open woodland in the South West.
- Height and spread: 25-40 m, sometimes 50 m (WP, EUCLID); typically 20-30 m in cultivation (ANBG). Spread from council and botanic-garden sheets (via search results): 8-20 m (Botanic Gardens of South Australia), 10-15 m (Wyndham), 15-25 m (Burnside). DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: tall, straight trunk; bark smooth throughout, pale, uniform or slightly mottled, white to pink or coppery (ANBG adds red or blue-grey), often powdery, shed in thin curling flakes in spring. Clear-bole fraction: not found.
- Crown: "a symmetrical spreading canopy of sparse foliage" (ANBG); "a conspicuously narrow-leaved crown" (EUCLID): high, open and light.
- Leaves: narrow-lanceolate to curved, 10-23 cm long x 0.6-2.8(3.5) cm wide, petiole 1-2.5 cm; glossy green, the same on both sides; lemon-scented when crushed.
- Flowers and fruit: white, in axillary compound clusters of 3-bud groups; ANBG mid-winter into spring, WP "most months". Fruit urn- or barrel-shaped, 0.8-1.5 cm long x 0.7-1.2 cm wide.
- Pruning and behaviour: grows up to 2 m a year; "branch-drop" is a possibility, so it needs space; one horticultural source (via search results) advises raising the canopy base.
- Sources: https://apps.lucidcentral.org/euclid/text/entities/corymbia_citriodora.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corymbia_citriodora ; https://anbg.gov.au/gnp/trainees-2016/corymbia-citriodora.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_Western_Australia ; via search results: https://plantselector.botanicgardens.sa.gov.au/Plants/Details/17829 , https://www.wyndham.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-10/Corymbia%20citriodora_C_17.pdf , https://burnside.sa.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/3/environment-amp-sustainability/trees/tree-species-fact-sheets/tree-species-fact-sheets-lemon-scented-gum.pdf

### Canary Island date palm — *Phoenix canariensis*

- Origin and Perth use: Canary Islands. (The Victorian Heritage Register notes a period of popularity of palms in the 1920s, especially this species.) In Perth: a century-old row at Perth Zoo, South Perth, lining the path from the station entrance; a listed specimen at the Leederville TAFE campus (City of Vincent significant tree list). Other Perth avenues: not found in the sources read.
- Height and spread: 10-20 m (WP; tallest recorded 36 m); 12-18 m high x 6-12 m crown width (40-60 ft x 20-40 ft; NCSU).
- Trunk: solitary, stout, columnar, 0.6-0.9 m in diameter (2-3 ft), greyish brown, patterned with diamond-shaped leaf-base scars.
- Crown: typically 75-125 living fronds (WP; NCSU "up to 100"), arching.
- Fronds: pinnate, 4-6 m long (WP; NCSU up to 4.6 m / 15 ft), with 80-100 leaflets on each side of the rachis; leaflets narrow, 30-45 cm long (12-18 in), stiff, deep green; very sharp spines 5-20 cm long (2-8 in) on the lower part of the petiole.
- Flowers and fruit: cream to pale yellow flowers on drooping panicles 0.9-1.2 m long in spring; fruit oval, yellow to orange, about 2 cm long x 1 cm, in large hanging clusters on female palms.
- Pruning: the lower, older fronds are routinely removed; the trimmed leaf bases below the crown form a swollen "nut" with a pineapple shape (hence "pineapple palm"). An untrimmed skirt of dead fronds: not described in the sources read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_canariensis ; https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/phoenix-canariensis/ ; https://perthzoo.wa.gov.au/article/long-live-the-trees (via search results) ; https://www.vincent.wa.gov.au/Profiles/vincent/Assets/ClientData/Documents/Develop_Build/Planning/Trees_of_Significance/TREES_OF_SIGNIFICANCE_-Updated_Nov_2016.pdf (via search results)

### Queensland box, brush box — *Lophostemon confertus*

- Origin and Perth use: north-east New South Wales and coastal Queensland; "commonly used as a street tree in Sydney, Melbourne, Perth". Commonly planted in Perth from the 1940s to the 1980s; more than 15% of the City of Stirling's street trees; a main species of South Perth's post-war plantings.
- Height and spread: forest tree to 40 m or more in the wild; as a street tree 10-15 m high x 10-15 m wide (City of Gosnells), or 15 m high with 10 m branch spread (Northern Beaches Council). DBH: not found.
- Trunk and bark: "the trunk has brown, slightly rough bark", with smoother, paler bark higher up (Gosnells).
- Crown: dome-shaped, dense, giving heavier shade than a eucalypt; rarely sheds limbs.
- Leaves: evergreen, dark green, leathery. Dimensions: not found.
- Flowers: white, in spring (Gosnells); not conspicuous.
- Pruning (important for the Perth look): "often requires lopping to accommodate overhead power lines, but survives pruning well" (WP). A lopped variant (reduced, flattened crown with dense regrowth) for the power-line side of a street and a full-dome variant for the other side is a reasonable reading of this, but the lopped shape itself is not described in the sources read.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lophostemon_confertus ; https://www.gosnells.wa.gov.au/City-Services/Community-Safety/In-your-street/Street-trees/Approved-Street-Trees ; https://www.northernbeaches.nsw.gov.au/environment/trees/brushbox ; https://www.stirling.wa.gov.au/waste-and-environment/trees/faqs-and-useful-documents

### Cape lilac, white cedar — *Melia azedarach*

- Origin and Perth use: Indomalaya and Australasia (not native to Perth); a main species of South Perth's post-war street plantings; naturalised and regarded as a weed in Western Australia, spread by birds.
- Height and spread: commonly 7-12 m (WP; exceptionally 45 m); 9-12 m high x 9-12 m wide (30-40 ft; NCSU). Short-lived, averaging about 20 years (WP).
- Trunk and bark: bark dark brown with lighter spots, with narrow furrows giving a striped look; reddish on young trees.
- Crown: rounded, with stiff coarse branches.
- Leaves: deciduous; alternate, long-stalked, two or three times pinnate, up to 50 cm long (NCSU 30-60 cm); leaflets up to 7 cm long, toothed, dark green above and lighter below; yellow in autumn before falling.
- Flowers: small (under 2.5 cm), fragrant, five pale purple / lilac petals, star-shaped, very numerous in panicles 20-40 cm long in the leaf axils, in spring (NCSU "spring and summer"); Perth months not found.
- Fruit (the winter signature): marble-sized round drupes 1-1.3 cm across, yellow-green ripening light yellow, "hanging on the tree all winter" on the bare branches, gradually wrinkling and turning almost white.
- Seasonal states: bare with yellow berry clusters in winter; lilac flowers in spring; green in summer; yellow in autumn.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melia_azedarach ; https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/melia-azedarach/

## 4. Ground and soil notes (for ground patches)

### Dune systems of the Swan Coastal Plain

- The plain is a series of dune systems running west to east in increasing age: Quindalup dunes at the coast, then Spearwood dunes, then Bassendean dunes, with the Pinjarra plain (alluvium) between the Bassendean dunes and the scarp. Soils are "fairly infertile" sands with wetlands held between the dunes.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Coastal_Plain

### Bassendean sand (banksia woodland floor)

- Sand: the oldest, most heavily leached dunes; "deep or very deep grey siliceous sands", non-calcareous, podsolised, with low-lying wet areas between the dunes; bleached quartz sand is the characteristic material. For a ground patch this reads as pale grey to white quartz sand (the darker grey of organic staining is an inference, not sourced). (A Royal Society of WA paper cautions that bright yellow subsoil indicates Spearwood, not Bassendean, sand.)
- Vegetation structure: a prominent low tree layer of banksia (B. attenuata, B. menziesii, also B. ilicifolia, B. prionotes, and B. littoralis in damp spots) with scattered eucalypts (jarrah, tuart, prickly bark E. todtiana); structure (height, cover, density) varies across the plain. Canopy cover percentages: not found.
- Ground layer: a highly diverse low sclerophyll shrub and sedge layer, 30-90 plant species per 100 m2. Named understorey species include Hibbertia hypericoides, Stirlingia latifolia, Adenanthos cygnorum (woollybush), Bossiaea eriocarpa, Conostephium pendulum, Eremaea pauciflora, Gompholobium tomentosum, Kunzea glabrescens, Petrophile linearis, Philotheca spicata, Allocasuarina humilis, balga (Xanthorrhoea preissii) and the tufted Phlebocarya ciliata; semaphore sedge (section 2) grows in white, grey or yellow sand. Shrub spacing, bare-sand fraction and litter depth: not found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banksia_Woodlands_of_the_Swan_Coastal_Plain ; via search results: https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/banksia-woodlands-scp-guide.pdf (timed out when fetched), https://peel-harvey.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/PeelHarveyCoastalMapUnitLegend.pdf , https://www.rswa.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/RSWA-103-p1-8-Turner-and-Laliberte.pdf , https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/FullTextFiles/211068.pdf

### Spearwood sand and limestone (tuart belt)

- Sand: younger, less leached dunes nearer the coast over Tamala Limestone; yellow deep sands (the colour from iron-oxide coatings on the grains), pale deep sands and yellow-brown shallow sands over limestone; the yellow or grey sand "may be several metres thick".
- Vegetation: tuart grows on coastal sands with limestone at depth (section 2); banksia woodland also occurs on deep Spearwood sands.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Coastal_Plain ; via search results: https://peel-harvey.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/PeelHarveyCoastalMapUnitLegend.pdf , https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/FullTextFiles/069730.pdf

### Quindalup dunes (beach and foredune)

- Sand: the youngest dunes; "deep pale cream calcareous" sand, also described as white calcareous sand, sometimes with limestone deposits.
- Vegetation: species-poor and structurally the simplest of the three dune systems. The first perennial colonisers and main builders of the foredunes are the grasses Spinifex hirsutus and S. longifolius; less abundant there are Scaevola crassifolia, Olearia axillaris, Acacia cyclopis, Pelargonium and spreading clumps of coast sword-sedge (Lepidosperma gladiatum). Erect woody species are kept stunted by wind pruning and spread sideways instead.
- Cover: one 2024 field plot at Kennedy Bay (a single site, not a regional figure) recorded Olearia axillaris, Scaevola crassifolia and Spinifex longifolius at 12% cover each. Regional cover fractions: not found (the standard reference, Semeniuk and others 1989, was not accessed).
- Sources (all via search results): https://archive.org/download/biostor-226317/biostor-226317.pdf , https://www.epa.wa.gov.au/sites/default/files/Referral_Documentation/Flora%20and%20Vegetation%20Assessment-KennedyBay2024.pdf , https://biodiversitylibrary.org/part/238034 , https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/FullTextFiles/069715.pdf

### Jarrah forest floor (Darling Range)

- Ground: Archaean granite and metamorphic rock capped by an extensive lateritic duricrust (caprock), dissected by later drainage. Uplands and slopes are mantled with lateritic gravels and duricrust; upland soils are shallow gravelly sands over duricrust, mid-slopes have sandy pisolitic (pea-gravel) gravel in the upper layers with duricrust close to the surface, and valley floors have deep soils. Granite crops out on the scarp. Soil colour and litter depth: not found in the sources read.
- Vegetation: jarrah and marri overstorey; a mid-storey of bull banksia, common sheoak, balga, Bossiaea aquifolium and Acacia species over a diverse understorey; heath is a common understorey in the north and east; zamia grows on these lateritic soils (section 2). Deep leaf litter and exfoliating bark are noted as habitat features that take more than eight years to return to rehabilitated forest.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarrah_Forest ; https://florabase.dbca.wa.gov.au/browse/profile/85 ; via search results: https://library.dbca.wa.gov.au/FullTextFiles/069171.pdf , https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/112292/1/Soltanghesi%2C%20George%20%26%20Tibbett%20%28Jarrah%20Forest%20Soils%20-%20LAND%29%202023.pdf

### River and wetland edge

- Soils: the Swan foreshore is generally Bassendean sand with areas of alluvium, peat, clay and swamp and river deposits; red podsolic and alluvial soils around Heirisson Island and Maylands; silty foreshore that erodes easily, with flood scour exposing tree roots. Lake and swamp margins have damp black sandy soils (jointed rush) and peat (modong in mound springs).
- Vegetation zones from the water up: a closed band of rushes at the very edge (shore rush Juncus kraussii, pale rush Juncus pallidus; samphire and saltmarsh in saline tidal flats); then fringing woodland of flooded gum and swamp paperbark, with patches of low open forest of swamp sheoak and saltwater paperbark where it is more saline. The understorey is commonly degraded and weedy; the exotic bulrush Typha orientalis is common.
- Sources (via search results): https://www.dbca.wa.gov.au/media/5965/download , https://www.dbca.wa.gov.au/media/5964/download , https://dbca.wa.gov.au/media/1153/download , https://archive.org/download/biostor-256710/biostor-256710.pdf ; and the species pages cited in section 2.
